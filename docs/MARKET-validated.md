# What the market actually says

## The thing that could have killed the "shared claim log" idea

**ClaimReview exists.** A schema.org standard for fact-check markup, in use across
1K–10K domains — PolitiFact, Full Fact, Maldita, Chequeado. A shared claim log is
not a new idea.

**It does not kill ours, and the difference is the whole product.** ClaimReview records
**a verdict about a public claim, entered manually by a journalist after publication**.
It is a *publishing* format for fact-check articles. It does not carry the verbatim
passage, the source's independence basis, or — decisively — **the refusals**. There is
no ClaimReview for *"this cannot be sourced, and here is what would resolve it."*

**So it is an export target, not a competitor.** Emitting ClaimReview from our log is
interoperability; it costs little and it means our records travel into a standard other
tools already read.

**Every one of these is a MARKETPLACE — they sell you data that is already cleared.**
None of them answers the question a rights-holder or a lab actually has about material
they already possess: *can I legally use THIS, and can I prove it?*

## The fact that changes the buyer

> **From 2026 the EU AI Act requires every AI company to disclose training data sources,
> respect copyright opt-outs, and label AI-generated content.**

That is a **compliance obligation with a date on it**, and the artifact it demands —
per-asset provenance, the instrument that governs each item, the ones that cannot be
cleared and why — is exactly what this engine already emits. `fixtures/gap-report-600.md`
is a draft of that document today.

## The positioning, corrected by the evidence

**Not** "AI fact-checker #6" — citations are table stakes and funded (Preuve, Factiverse,
Provenance, Google Backstory).

**Not** a data marketplace — Troveo, Human Archive and Deeptune are there with money.

**The compliance evidence layer for training-data provenance.** Marketplaces sell cleared
data. We prove clearance on data you already hold, per asset, per use, with the
instrument quoted verbatim — and we say which items cannot be cleared at all, which is
the half a marketplace has no reason to build and a fact-checker cannot produce.

**Buyers:** rights-holders proving what they can sell into the licensing market · AI labs
facing the disclosure obligation · studios and archives clearing their own libraries ·
E&O underwriters pricing what a production asserts.

## The moat, stated precisely

Anyone can rebuild "what is true" — that is a search. **Nobody else is accumulating what
CANNOT be proven, and why**, because no competing product emits refusals at all. Every
production that runs through this leaves behind both halves: the cleared claims *and* the
map of what the open web cannot settle.

**The negative space is the asset.** It is the one thing that cannot be recreated by
someone with a better search engine.
