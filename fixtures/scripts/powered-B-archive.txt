NARRATOR (V.O.)

Ask an archivist what they hold and they will tell you a number. Ask what they can use
and the number collapses.

The British Library has estimated that forty percent of its copyrighted collection is
orphaned. A study in April 2009 put the collections of UK public sector organisations at
roughly 25 million orphan works. The BFI National Archive holds more than 275,000 titles,
including around 210,000 television programmes, with material dating from 1894.

Europeana, the EU's aggregated cultural platform, carries over 50 million items from
more than 3,000 institutions, and every item must carry a rights statement drawn from a
fixed vocabulary. That vocabulary is rightsstatements.org, developed jointly by Europeana
and the Digital Public Library of America, and it defines twelve standardised statements.
One of them, In Copyright — EU Orphan Work, exists specifically for works cleared under
Directive 2012/28/EU. Another, Copyright Not Evaluated, means the holding institution has
never assessed the item's status at all.

The mechanism those institutions rely on is the diligent search, defined by the Orphan
Works Directive, which member states had to implement by 29 October 2014. Cleared works
are recorded in the EU orphan works register held by the European Union Intellectual
Property Office.

In Britain the practical route is the Intellectual Property Office scheme, opened in
October 2014, which grants licences of up to seven years. Fewer than one hundred were
granted in its first year, against a national estimate of 25 million orphans — which is
the whole problem in one ratio.

Across the Atlantic the archives have no scheme to use. The Copyright Office published
Orphan Works and Mass Digitization in June 2015 and nothing followed it. The Google Books
settlement, which would have built a registry, was rejected by a federal judge in 2011.
The Internet Archive holds millions of moving image files, many with no traceable owner.
Canada's Copyright Board, working under Section 77, had issued 321 orphan work licences
by June 2023 — the only body anywhere issuing them at volume.
