NARRATOR (V.O.)

In May 1936 the Resettlement Administration released The Plow That Broke the Plains,
a Pare Lorentz film that cost $19,260 to produce. It was seen by ten million people
in 1937 alone.

The film said the Great Plains had seemed inexhaustible — four hundred million acres —
yet in fifty years a part of it had been turned into a Dust Bowl. It blamed too many
cattle and sheep, land plowed that should have been left in grass, and machines that
let one person open many acres.

At the end the narrator asked what America was going to do about forty million acres
totally ruined and another two hundred million badly damaged.
