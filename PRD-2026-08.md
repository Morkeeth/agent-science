## 1. One-liner, problem, and the dated wedge

**Agent Science extracts every checkable claim from a script or report, sources each one
verbatim from a fetched document, and REFUSES the ones it cannot — printing the reason
instead of inventing a citation.**

**The problem.** A factual production — a documentary, a report, a training-data
disclosure — cannot be insured or defended until every claim carries a source. Today that
is hand-work by a researcher, and a single miss is a lawsuit or a false regulatory filing.
The failure mode is not "wrong answer"; it is a *confident* answer with a citation that
does not actually state the claim, or that traces back to the claim's own origin.

**The wedge — the EU AI Act made one version of this filing mandatory, with a date and a
penalty.** The European Commission's AI Office published the mandatory template for the
public summary of GPAI training content on 24 July 2025, implementing Article 53(1)(d) of
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689
([EC AI Office FAQ + template](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/template-general-purpose-ai-model-providers-summarise-their-training-content)).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Obligation live since** | 2 August 2025 |
| **Enforcement / verification begins** | **2 August 2026** — the AI Office may verify compliance and issue corrective measures ([KLA analysis](https://kla.digital/blog/eu-ai-act-august-2026-what-still-applies)) |
| **Penalty** | up to **3% of worldwide turnover, or EUR 15,000,000, whichever is higher** ([EC FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/template-general-purpose-ai-model-providers-summarise-their-training-content)) |
| **Cadence** | updated at least **every six months**, or sooner on a material change |
| **Legacy models** | pre-2 Aug 2025 models have until 2 Aug 2027 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/european-commission-releases-mandatory-template-for-public-disclosure-of-ai-training-data)) |

**The honest half of the wedge.** The filing itself is a *narrative* summary, not
item-by-item — deliberately, to protect trade secrets. **No regulator reads a per-item
annex.** So we do NOT sell "we generate your disclosure"; that is a paragraph anyone can
write. The regulation requires stating *to what extent* lawful text-and-data-mining
conditions were respected — and **an extent cannot be stated truthfully without being
measured.** We produce the record that the measurement was performed. Anyone can write the
summary; nobody else can substantiate one.

## 2. Day-two buyer (not a judge)

The judge runs `agent_science.py` on a documentary script. That is the demo, not the
customer. The **day-two buyer** is whoever must sign a provenance statement they can be
fined or sued for:

1. **A GPAI provider's compliance / legal counsel** (primary, dated). They must publish an
   Article 53(1)(d) summary by enforcement and re-file every six months. They need an
   auditable evidence trail behind one narrative sentence — the record that clearance was
   actually attempted per item, with the undetermined items named rather than rounded up
   to "cleared." Budget: compliance line, recurring, not a one-off tool spend.
2. **A documentary producer / broadcaster's clearance desk** (the original wedge). They
   pay for E&O insurance and cannot air an unsourced claim. The corpus compounds across
   every production on a subject, so the desk's cost falls as its back-catalogue grows.

Both share one shape: a person who is liable for a claim and needs proof, on a recurring
cadence, that the checking happened — including proof of the claims that *could not* be
cleared. That last part is what they cannot buy anywhere else.

## 3. What EXISTS vs what is claimed

Verified 2026-08-24 by re-running the harness and re-reading the source, not from the
README's prose.

**Live and verified:**
- **Hosted, 3/3 partner integrations live.** `agent-science-33kamss2jq-uc.a.run.app`
  returns HTTP 200; `/health` reports `gemini_path: vertex:hack-fleet`, `parallel: true`,
  `agent_builder: true` (ADK 2.7.1), `engine_default: adk`. Every gap report stamps which
  engine answered, so "the agent ran" is a field a judge reads, not a claim.
- **The refusal is structural, not conventional.** `Verdict.__post_init__` cannot build a
  GREEN/RED/DISPUTED without a `citation_url` and `quoted_terms` (`clearance/verdict.py`).
  `clearance/verify.py` refuses any passage not occurring *verbatim* in the fetched
  document, not carrying the claim's distinctive term, or not reading as a statement —
  structural checks only.
- **Held-out refusal control, watched going red.** `tests/test_watch_it_go_red.py` →
  **72 passed, 0 failed**, and it asserts that a *refuse-everything* locator FAILS the
  held-out supported set. A guard that only watches one direction is not a guard.
- **Source independence as a property of the SET.** `clearance/independence.py` collapses
  mirrors and caches to one `origin_key`, classifies primary / derived / unclassified, and
  prints it beside each verdict — never promoting an unclassified source to primary.
- **Article 53 evidence annex.** `clearance/article53.py` + `fixtures/ARTICLE-53-ANNEX.md`:
  600 items assessed, **524 (87%) carry a rights-reserving instrument**, each named.
- **Corpus-verify loop (the dogfood).** `clear_corpus.py` parses **137 claims** from
  `research-corpus/` deterministically (offline), and `verify_corpus` runs each through the
  same engine that clears a script — a research finding clears only if its own cited source
  verbatim states it.

**Claimed but qualified — do not inflate:**
- README says "71 passed" and "56 claims"; the suite is now **72 passed** and the corpus
  parses **137 claims**. The *compounding curve* was measured on 56 (four scripts, one
  subject) — that measurement has not been re-run at 137.
- The corpus-verify loop's `verify_corpus` is the live half and needs network; only
  `parse_corpus` (137) is verified offline here. The full fetch-and-clear over all 137 was
  not run in this pass.
- "The corpus compounds" is measured same-subject (0→20→39→46% reuse), not at
  GPAI-dataset scale.

## 4. Wedge vs competitors

Four funded companies rebuild *what is true*. None emit refusals, and none classify
source independence.

| | What they do | What they do NOT do |
|---|---|---|
| **ClaimReview** (schema.org) | a markup standard for publishing fact-check verdicts | verifies nothing itself; it is a format, not an engine |
| **Factiverse** | stance detection + a database of 350k+ fact-checks ([API](https://www.factiverse.ai/solutions/api)) | returns a *stance/signal*; no refuse-or-verify verdict, no independence classification, no "unsourceable" |
| **Patronus (Lynx)** | RAG hallucination detection, ~$10-20/1k calls ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/patronus-ai-launches-worlds-first-self-serve-api-to-stop-ai-hallucinations)) | scores a *probability* of hallucination; downweights, never refuses; no provenance record |

**Three things none of them do:**
1. **Refuse, not downweight.** A confidence score of 0.6 still ships the claim. A verdict
   of UNSOURCED does not. For a liable buyer, the two errors cost differently: promoting a
   blog to primary gets a production sued; demoting a real source is a two-minute human
   override the report already shows. We are asymmetric on purpose.
2. **Independence classification.** Three citations to one origin is one citation. On the
   demo this *demoted* 3 of 6 sourced claims — a worse number, and the true one.
3. **The compounding corpus / refusal ledger.** The asset is the *negative space*: what
   CANNOT be proven, and why. A refusal is expensive to produce (a full search, documents
   fetched and read, an independence assessment) and it is the one half a fact-check
   marketplace has no reason to build. Absence is not something you find by searching
   harder.

## 5. Ambitious vision — the compliance-evidence company

Not a fact-checker. **The system of record for provenance you can be fined for.**

The same `Verdict` shape serves two nouns — a *fact* with no source and an *asset* with no
rights instrument are one record. That is the expansion path: every regime that will soon
require *proof that provenance was checked* is a shelf on the same desk —

- **GPAI training-data disclosure** (EU AI Act Art. 53, live now, the beachhead).
- **Documentary / news E&O clearance** (the original wedge, recurring per production).
- **Dataset provenance for enterprise AI procurement** (buyers demanding a cleared-data
  attestation before they license a model).

The moat is the **cross-production ledger**: a claim proven — or proven *unprovable* —
once is free for everyone afterward, and the refusal half is the part no competitor
accumulates. The endgame is the dossier a lawyer signs, backed by a ledger that remembers
every attempt across every filing, so the fourth six-month re-filing costs a fraction of
the first.

## 6. Build plan (ordered by leverage)

1. **Wire the cross-production ledger into the live pipeline.** `clearance/ledger.py`
   exists (the refusal ledger, the "free for everyone afterward" object) but is NOT
   imported by `agent_science.py` or the hosted service — the live path uses only the
   per-subject corpus. **This is the highest-leverage slice: the moat is written but
   unplugged.** Route recall/remember through the ledger, so refusals and clears compound
   across subjects, not just within one.
2. **The dossier artifact a lawyer signs.** Turn the Article 53 annex + gap report into one
   signed, dated, per-item evidence dossier: every claim, its verdict, its quoted source,
   its independence class, and the named undetermined items. This is the object the buyer
   actually pays for.
3. **Real-scale corpus.** Re-run the compounding measurement on a real GPAI-style dataset
   (hundreds of thousands of items), not 56 claims — the curve is the pitch and it is
   currently measured at toy scale.
4. **The corpus-verify loop as the standing dogfood.** Run `verify_corpus` over all 137
   research claims in CI; a research finding that fails to clear against its own cited
   source is a red build. The product checking its own evidence is the demo *and* the
   regression net.
5. **Multi-tenant.** Per-customer subject shelves and ledgers, auth, and isolation, so two
   providers' corpora do not leak into each other. Prerequisite to selling.

## 7. Honest gaps

- **The two halves do not share a live ledger.** Fact-leg and rights/asset-leg share the
  `Verdict` constructor, but the cross-production `ledger.py` is unwired (see build slice
  #1). Today "compounding" means same-subject corpus only.
- **Wrong-refusal, off-claim-verbatim, and circular-sourcing are open by design.** Nothing
  yet catches a *wrong* refusal, a passage that is verbatim and on-topic yet does not state
  the claim, or a source that is the claim's own origin
  (`docs/FINDING-refusal-correctness.md`, `-substring-is-not-a-statement.md`,
  `-circular-sourcing.md`). These are flagged, not solved.
- **API cost is per-claim and flat.** Reuse rises but per-claim spend does not fall
  (~$0.003-0.004); the corpus removes the *easy* claims first, leaving hard ones that need
  a second escalation search. The saving is researcher hours, invisible in the API bill.
- **Unmeasured at scale.** The compounding curve is 56 claims on one subject. Fetch
  reliability, rate limits, and cost at 100k+ items are untested. `verify_corpus` over all
  137 corpus claims was not run in this pass — only the offline parse (137) is verified.
- **ADK dependency.** `engine_default: adk` is a submission requirement; if the ADK path
  fails the service silently falls back to `direct` and stamps `adk_error`. Live today, but
  a second runtime to keep green.
